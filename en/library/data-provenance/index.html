Data provenance: knowing where your chart's numbers come from

Data provenance means knowing how each number on a chart is produced: real exchange volume, a broker's tick volume, reference volume from another market, or an estimate.

IntermediateAURORA StandardAURORA Pro8 min read
Topic: Volume, Sessions & DataMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

The word "volume" appears on both a gold chart and a stock chart: is it really the same thing?

After this lesson you will

  • Tell exchange volume, tick volume, reference volume and estimated Delta apart
  • Know why results can differ between brokers and data sources
  • Read the data-source labels on the AURORA dashboard

What is data provenance?

Every number on a chart has a provenance: who produced it, what it measures, and what its limits are. For volume and money flow, the common sources are:

Kind of data Where you find it What it measures How usable
Exchange volume Stocks, crypto, futures Real traded volume Fully: RVOL, VWAP, CMF, Delta
Tick volume Forex, many broker CFDs Number of price changes Relative terms only (RVOL)
Reference volume Gold and index CFDs (borrowed from futures) Volume of a related market Useful, but not the CFD’s own volume
Estimated Delta / CVD Calculated from lower-timeframe candles Estimated aggressive buying vs selling Reference only, not native bid / ask data
Open Interest Crypto perpetuals, futures Open contracts Combined with price to read new money flow

Beyond volume, higher-timeframe data has a provenance issue too: an unfinished higher-timeframe bar keeps changing, so using it historically easily “sees the future”.

Why does data provenance matter?

  • Same name, different nature. “Volume” on a forex chart and on a stock chart are completely different things.
  • Results differ between brokers. Prices, candle close times, spreads and tick volume vary by broker, so the same strategy can produce different statistics.
  • Avoiding false conclusions. A volume filter that “works” on tick volume may only reflect the broker’s activity hours, not real money flow.

Common misunderstandings

  • Tick volume is real money flow. No. It correlates with activity but does not measure traded volume.
  • Futures volume is the CFD’s volume. It is the volume of a related market, with its own trading hours and roll dates.
  • Delta on TradingView is native order flow. Delta on candlestick charts is an estimate, limited by how much lower-timeframe data your plan allows.
  • Data is the same at every source. Continuous futures symbols can show price gaps at contract rolls; two brokers’ CFD prices can differ.

How does AURORA label its data sources?

  • The Market Preset decides the volume source:
    • STOCK: full real volume.
    • FOREX: RVOL from tick volume only.
    • CFD: reference volume from futures.
    • CRYPTO: exchange volume + Open Interest.
  • External volume for CFDs:
    • AURORA Standard V12.2: requires entering the symbol and ticking a confirmation box. The dashboard shows EXT !CONFIRM (not confirmed), EXT ✓ (data available) or EXT NO DATA.
    • AURORA Pro: picks the contract from the chart symbol automatically, for example XAU → COMEX:GC1!. An unrecognised symbol shows EXT ? and volume is switched off, rather than using the wrong symbol.
  • No mixing of sources: in external-volume mode, a bar without external data is treated as having no volume; chart volume is not used to fill the gap, because the two sources have different scales. The score loses 5 points when external volume cannot be loaded.
  • Reference metrics are marked: with external volume, the tooltip shows pVWAP, pCMF instead of VWAP, CMF.
  • Delta is labelled as a lower-timeframe estimate. AURORA Pro uses it for CVD in Tier 3.
  • Higher-timeframe data comes only from closed bars. Signals confirm on bar close by default.
  • RVOL~ on the dashboard is a projection for the live candle, not its final value.

What a candlestick chart cannot tell you

  • The order book and who is trading. Not on a candlestick chart.
  • Off-exchange data. Trades that do not go through an exchange (OTC) are not shown.
  • Your broker’s execution quality. Real slippage and latency only show up in your own account.
  • So: always state the symbol, exchange or broker and timeframe when sharing results, and validate on the exact data source you trade. Historical results, before costs.

Practical checklist

  • Know whether the volume on your chart is exchange volume, tick volume or reference volume.
  • Do not compare results from two brokers as if they were the same data.
  • For CFDs, confirm that the reference futures symbol matches the market you trade.
  • Read Delta and CVD as estimates, not native order flow.
  • Check the data status on the dashboard before trusting volume metrics.
