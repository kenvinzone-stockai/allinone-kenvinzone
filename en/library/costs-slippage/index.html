Commission and slippage: how a small edge disappears

Commission and slippage are deducted from every trade; in R, cost equals the round-trip cost divided by the stop distance, so the tighter the stop, the more costs eat the edge.

IntermediateAURORA StandardAURORA Pro6 min read
Topic: Research & ValidationMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

A system shows +0.15R per trade on the chart: what is left after commission and slippage?

After this lesson you will

  • List the cost components of a trade
  • Convert costs into R using the stop distance
  • Set commission and slippage correctly in the Strategy Validator

What does a trade cost?

Cost Common in Notes
Commission Stocks, crypto (percentage), futures (per contract) Paid on every fill, including partial exits
Spread Forex, CFDs The bid / ask gap; widens around news and in quiet hours
Slippage Every market The fill differs from the intended price; larger in fast markets
Overnight financing (swap) CFDs, forex, crypto perpetuals (funding) Charged when holding overnight

Converting costs into R

Cost per trade (R) = Total round-trip cost ÷ Stop distance

Example: a system with +0.15R expectancy before costs and a round-trip cost of 0.30 price units:

Stop distance Cost in R Expectancy after costs
6 0.05R +0.10R
3 0.10R +0.05R
1.5 0.20R −0.05R
1 0.30R −0.15R

Same system, same costs. Only because the stop is tighter, the edge becomes a loss.

Why does it matter?

  • Most edges are small. A few tenths of an R per trade is normal; costs can eat all of it.
  • Tight stops look attractive because of large size. But costs in R grow in proportion.
  • More trades multiply costs. High-frequency systems on low timeframes are the most cost-sensitive.

Common misunderstandings

  • Fees are small and negligible. In R they become obvious; 0.1R per trade can be two thirds of the expectancy.
  • Spreads are fixed. Spreads widen sharply around news, session opens and quiet hours.
  • Limit orders have no slippage. Limit orders do not fill worse than their price, but may not fill at all when price only just touches them.
  • Backtests without costs are fine for comparison. Comparing configurations with different trade frequencies requires costs.

Setting costs in AURORA’s Strategy Validator

  • The Validator’s defaults are 0.05% per trade + 2 ticks of slippage: placeholders only. Open Strategy Properties and enter real figures:
    • Stocks, crypto: a percentage commission.
    • CME / COMEX futures: a fee per contract.
    • CFDs, forex: mostly the spread; set slippage high enough to reflect the average spread plus slippage.
  • Pullback limit orders: the Verify price for limit orders setting. Compare 0 ticks with 1 tick or more to see how the fill assumption changes results.
  • Partial exits: 50 / 50 or 50 / 25 / 25 plans create more fills; with brokers that charge a minimum per order, costs rise.
  • The chart dashboard excludes costs. Always use the Validator for figures after costs before trading real money.

Limits

  • Real costs vary over time, especially spreads around news; an average is only an approximation.
  • Overnight financing depends on the broker and product, and is rarely fully modelled in backtests.
  • Testing live with small size is the only way to know your exact costs.

Practical checklist

  • Know every cost of what you trade: commission, spread, slippage, overnight financing.
  • Convert the total round-trip cost into R: cost ÷ stop distance.
  • Be careful with very tight stops: cost in R shoots up.
  • Replace the Strategy Validator's default costs with your broker's real figures.
  • Judge systems by results after costs.

A system shows +0.15R per trade on the chart. But every trade pays commission, spread and slippage. The simple conversion: cost in R equals the round-trip cost divided by the stop distance. With a cost of 0.3 price units, a stop of 6 means 0.05R, while a stop of 1.5 means 0.2R. So for the same system with the same costs, just tightening the stop turns a +0.15R edge into a loss. That is why you should always judge a system after costs, and set the Strategy Validator's costs to match your broker.
