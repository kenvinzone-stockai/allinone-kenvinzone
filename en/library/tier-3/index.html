Tier 3: traces of large money flow around a setup

Tier 3 is AURORA Pro's context layer, looking for traces of large money flow around a setup: fresh FVGs, liquidity sweeps, CVD and absorption, plus suitability for holding a Runner.

AdvancedAURORA Pro9 min read
Topic: AURORA Pro & SMCMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

A breakout happens right after price sweeps an old low: does that make the setup more trustworthy?

After this lesson you will

  • Know the four Tier 3 components and what each measures
  • Understand data coverage and why Tier 3 does not decide when data is missing
  • Read the 💎 label and the Tier 3 and Sweep rows on the dashboard

What is Tier 3?

AURORA Pro scores in separate layers:

  1. Score: the market context.
  2. Setup Quality: the quality of the setup itself.
  3. Tier 3, or Institutional Quality: traces often associated with large money flow around the setup.

Tier 3 has four components:

Component What it measures
① Fresh FVG A same-direction FVG that formed recently (within 8 bars by default), is large enough (0.2 ATR or more) and has not been filled to its midpoint
② Liquidity sweep Price just swept a recent high / low and closed back inside (searched over 15 bars, valid for 8 bars, decaying over time)
③④ CVD and absorption Whether cumulative CVD leans towards the trade direction; whether there are signs of the other side being absorbed
Runner suitability Whether the trend has enough efficiency, strength, higher-timeframe agreement and AVWAP location to hold a long-running part of the position

Weights differ between BREAK and SUNNY: for BREAK, the FVG and the sweep matter more; for SUNNY, flow and Runner suitability matter more.

Data coverage: the share of components that actually had data at the setup. 100% means all four did; 50% means only two.

Classes: S from 85, A from 75, B from 60, C below 60.

Why do these traces deserve attention?

  • Sweep, then breakout suggests liquidity on the other side was used up before price moved in the setup’s direction.
  • A fresh FVG shows the last push was strong enough to leave a price gap, and that zone may serve as a pullback level.
  • CVD and absorption indicate whether (estimated) aggressive buying and selling support the trade direction.
  • Runner suitability helps decide whether to keep part of the position for a long trend run.

Common misunderstandings

  • Tier 3 sees institutional orders. No. A candlestick chart shows neither the order book nor who is trading. “Institutional” refers to patterns commonly attributed to large money flow.
  • CVD on TradingView is a real footprint. Delta and CVD in AURORA are estimated from lower-timeframe bars, not native bid / ask exchange data.
  • A Tier 3 score is meaningful even with missing data. A score built on a single component is easy to misread; that is why Filter mode requires a minimum coverage.
  • A 💎 always means better. It is a hypothesis to test on your data, not a default truth.

How does AURORA Pro use Tier 3?

  • Three modes (the Tier 3 mode box):
    • Off.
    • Stats only (default): scores and records, signals unchanged.
    • Filter (experimental): blocks setups below the threshold, 65 for Break and 60 for SUNNY by default, with minimum coverage of 50%.
  • The 💎 label replaces ⚡ on a BREAK when a liquidity sweep happened just before.
  • Display:
    • The dashboard: a 💎 Tier 3 row with the current score for BUY and SELL. The Full layout adds a 💎 Sweep row comparing the average R of Breaks with and without a sweep.
    • JSON: the tier3_quality and sweep fields.
    • Strategy Validator: trade names, for example BREAK-L Q72 T65.
  • FVG CE option for Pullback: places the limit order at the midpoint of a fresh FVG instead of a percentage; off by default.
  • Runner suitability drives the Tier3 adaptive Runner mode: the Runner is kept only when this score is 70 or above at entry.

Limits of Tier 3

  • Estimated data. CVD and absorption depend on how much lower-timeframe data your TradingView plan allows, and on the market’s volume quality (forex tick volume, reference volume for CFDs).
  • Interpretive patterns. FVGs and sweeps are defined by fixed rules, but the concepts themselves remain chart interpretations.
  • Small samples. Breaks with a sweep are relatively rare; the 💎 Sweep row needs time to collect enough trades.
  • Validate before filtering. Switch on Filter only when high-score groups show clearly better average R across many symbols and out-of-sample data. Historical results, before costs.

Practical checklist

  • Read Tier 3 as extra context, not as an entry condition.
  • Check the data coverage (Cov): below 50% the score means little.
  • Compare the 💎 Sweep row with the no-sweep row on the dashboard.
  • Remember that CVD on TradingView is an estimate, not native bid / ask data.
  • Switch on Filter only after validating across many symbols and out-of-sample data.

A breakout happens right after price sweeps an old low and snaps back. To traders who watch liquidity, that suggests sellers were absorbed before the trend really moved. Tier 3 gathers traces like these into a separate scoring layer: a fresh, unfilled FVG, a liquidity sweep just before the setup, CVD and absorption, and how suitable the trend is for holding a Runner. Every Tier 3 score comes with a data coverage figure: how many components actually had data. With low coverage, Tier 3 does not have enough basis to decide anything.
