How do you size a position?

Position sizing works back from the money you accept to lose and the distance to the stop, instead of trading the same fixed size on every trade.

BeginnerAURORA StandardAURORA Pro7 min read
Topic: Risk & Position SizingMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

Both trades were 0.1 lot: why did one lose 30 USD and the other 300 USD at the stop?

After this lesson you will

  • Apply the formula size = money at risk ÷ (stop distance × value per point)
  • Size trades for gold, EURUSD and Vietnamese stocks
  • Understand why leverage does not change risk when size is correct

What is position sizing?

Position sizing decides how much to trade so that if the stop is hit, you lose exactly the amount you planned:

Size = Money at risk ÷ (Stop distance × Value per point)

  • Money at risk = equity × risk per trade. This is 1R in money.
  • Stop distance = |Entry − Stop|.
  • Value per point = the profit / loss when price moves 1 unit, for 1 unit of size (1 lot, 1 contract, 1 share).

Worked examples

Market Money at risk Stop distance Value per point Size
Gold (XAUUSD) 100 USD (1% of 10,000) 12 USD 100 USD per lot per 1 USD (100-ounce lot) 100 ÷ 1,200 = 0.083 → 0.08 lot
EURUSD 100 USD 25 pips 10 USD per pip per standard lot 100 ÷ 250 = 0.4 lot
Vietnamese stock 1,000,000 ₫ (1% of 100 million) 25,000 − 23,500 = 1,500 ₫ 1 ₫ per share 666 → 600 shares (lots of 100)

Value per point can differ between brokers (gold lot size, account currency). Check the contract specifications on your platform.

Why not trade a fixed size?

  • Risk changes with the stop. The same 0.1 lot of gold loses 30 USD with a 3-USD stop and 300 USD with a 30-USD stop.
  • Statistics in R only make sense this way. When every loss costs exactly 1R, trade results are comparable.
  • Losing streaks stay under control. At 1% risk per trade, 10 losses in a row cut equity by about 9.6%, not half the account.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the size first and then a stop that “fits”. The stop is no longer an invalidation level.
  • Rounding up. Always round down to the lot step; rounding up means more risk than planned.
  • Thinking leverage raises risk. Leverage only reduces the margin you use. Risk is set by size and stop. What you must check is that the liquidation price sits beyond the stop.
  • Forgetting costs and slippage. With tight stops, costs push the real loss above 1R.

How does AURORA help with sizing?

  • The trade plan gives the stop distance: the F-SL label shows the price and the % distance from entry; the trade detail card shows Entry, F-SL, TP1, TP2.
  • The 🛡 T-SL label on the right edge shows the % risk of entering at the current price.
  • The Strategy Validator sizes trades as % of equity at risk (1% by default), with lot-step rounding and a leverage cap. It is the closest view of real trading.
  • When the stop is wide (for example an SMC ⚠SL label), size must shrink accordingly.

AURORA does not place orders or size trades in your account; the final number depends on your broker’s contract specifications.

Limits

  • Gaps and slippage can push the loss beyond 1R.
  • Minimum size. With a small account or a very wide stop, the correct size may be below the broker’s minimum; then skip the trade.
  • What risk % is sensible depends on your strategy and the drawdown you can tolerate; see the Drawdown article.

Practical checklist

  • Choose your risk per trade first (often 0.5–1% of equity) and keep it fixed.
  • Set the stop first, then calculate the size.
  • Know the value per point (pip, USD, share) of what you trade.
  • Round size down to the broker's lot step, never up.
  • Check that the liquidation price (with leverage) sits beyond the stop.

Both trades were 0.1 lot, yet one lost 30 dollars and the other 300 at the stop. The reason is different stop distances. A fixed size means risk jumps around. The right way works backwards: decide first how much money you accept to lose, for example 1% of equity, then divide by the stop distance times the value per point. With a 10,000-dollar account, 1% risk is 100 dollars. Gold with a 12-dollar stop gives about 0.08 lot. EURUSD with a 25-pip stop gives 0.4 lot. Leverage only changes the margin you post, not the money you lose at the stop, as long as the size is calculated correctly.
