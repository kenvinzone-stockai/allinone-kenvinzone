Structural stops: placing the stop where the idea is really wrong

A structural stop sits at the setup's own invalidation level, such as beyond an FVG zone or beyond a sweep's extreme, rather than at a general trailing line.

AdvancedAURORA Pro6 min read
Topic: AURORA Pro & SMCMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

If price breaks through the setup's zone the idea is already wrong, so why wait for a Trailing line further away to cut the loss?

After this lesson you will

  • Tell structural stops apart from Trailing-line stops
  • Know where AURORA Pro places the stop for each SMC setup
  • Understand the Warn only / Filter policy and how to resize when the stop is wide

What is a structural stop?

A structural stop sits at the price where, if touched, the setup’s own structure has failed: beyond the FVG zone, beyond the Order Block, beyond the sweep’s extreme. A small buffer is added so noise does not tag it.

Compared with a Trailing-line stop:

Trailing-line stop Structural stop
Used for BREAK, SUNNY SMC RETEST, SWEEP, IFVG
Placed at The Trailing line value at the signal candle Beyond the setup’s zone or extreme
Logic Exit when the short-term trend flips Exit when the setup’s structure fails

Why do SMC setups need their own stop?

  • The idea fails earlier. An SMC zone often sits closer to price than the Trailing line; waiting for the line means a bigger loss than necessary.
  • A more accurate 1R. Only a stop that reflects the setup makes that setup’s R statistics meaningful.
  • Sometimes it is wider. A deep sweep puts the extreme far away; then the structural stop is wider than the Trailing line, and sizing needs care.

Common misunderstandings

  • Structural stops are always tight. No. Deep sweeps or large zones create wide stops.
  • A wide stop means big risk. Money at risk depends on size. With correct position sizing, a wide stop still loses exactly 1R.
  • Right at the zone edge is optimal. Without a buffer, a small wick easily tags the stop.
  • ⚠SL means skip. The warning is for you to weigh, not a ban.

How does AURORA Pro handle structural stops?

  • Placement:
    • RETEST and IFVG: 0.1 ATR beyond the zone edge.
    • SWEEP: 0.1 ATR beyond the sweep extreme.
  • At least 0.3 ATR from the entry, so 1R is not tiny relative to costs and noise.
  • Wide-stop policy (the SMC structural SL policy box):
    • Off: width is not checked.
    • Warn only (default): the trade is still taken and the label carries ⚠SL. The tooltip shows the width, for example ⚠SL 4.8 ATR.
    • Filter: blocks setups whose stop is wider than the threshold. A ⛔ SMC label shows the reason “structural SL > 4 ATR”.
  • Default threshold 4 ATR, adjustable.
  • Independent of the Entry Filter Profile: the profile’s stop limit applies to Break only; SMC uses this separate policy.
  • The Strategy Validator tags wide-stop SMC trades with W, for example SMC-SWEEP-L W, so you can compare wide-stop and normal-stop groups.

Limits

  • Manual sizing errors. With ⚠SL, the correct size may fall below your broker’s minimum.
  • Gaps jump any stop. In gapping markets the real loss can exceed 1R.
  • 4 ATR is a starting point, not an optimum for every market. Compare the W group with the normal group before switching on Filter. Historical results, before costs.

Practical checklist

  • Place the stop where, if touched, the setup's idea is clearly wrong.
  • Add a small buffer beyond the zone so noise does not tag it.
  • Size from the stop distance: a wider stop means a smaller position.
  • When you see ⚠SL, consider smaller size or skipping rather than normal size.
  • Switch to Filter only after comparing wide-stop and normal-stop groups in the Validator.

For an SMC setup, if price closes through the setup's zone, the idea is already wrong. Waiting for a Trailing line further away means taking on unnecessary risk. That is why SMC in AURORA Pro uses structural stops: beyond the zone edge for RETEST and IFVG, beyond the sweep extreme for SWEEP, plus a small buffer. Structural stops are sometimes very wide, for example after a deep sweep. When wider than 4 ATR, the label shows a ⚠SL warning. By default AURORA only warns; it does not block. The most important rule: a wider stop needs a smaller size, so the money at risk stays exactly as planned.
