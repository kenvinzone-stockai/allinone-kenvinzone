Master AURORA Pro

Rule-based SMC, the quality layers and validating before you switch on a filter.

Advanced13 lessonsabout 2.2 h13 / 13 published

  1. Entry Filter Profile: choosing how selective setups are

    The Entry Filter Profile is the AURORA Pro setting that decides which setups qualify for entry, through a minimum score and a maximum Break stop distance, independently of trade management.

  2. Setup Quality: scoring each BREAK and SUNNY on its own

    Setup Quality is a score for each individual BREAK or SUNNY setup, measuring compression then expansion, candle decisiveness, path efficiency and location in the trend.

  3. Tier 3: traces of large money flow around a setup

    Tier 3 is AURORA Pro's context layer, looking for traces of large money flow around a setup: fresh FVGs, liquidity sweeps, CVD and absorption, plus suitability for holding a Runner.

  4. Where is the liquidity on a chart?

    On a chart, liquidity usually means areas where resting orders cluster around obvious highs and lows; price often sweeps them before continuing or reversing.

  5. SMC RETEST: entering when price returns to the breakout's zone

    SMC RETEST is a setup that enters when price returns into the FVG (or Order Block) left by a Break, and a candle closes back beyond the zone midpoint in the trend direction.

  6. What is an SMC Sweep + MSS?

    A Sweep + MSS is when price sweeps the low (or high) of a pullback and then closes beyond the latest pullback high (or low), showing momentum has returned with the main trend.

  7. What is an IFVG? When a price gap flips its role

    An IFVG (Inverse FVG) is an opposite-direction FVG that price closes through and that flips role: former resistance becomes support, and vice versa.

  8. Structural stops: placing the stop where the idea is really wrong

    A structural stop sits at the setup's own invalidation level, such as beyond an FVG zone or beyond a sweep's extreme, rather than at a general trailing line.

  9. SMC Quality: scoring SMC setups

    SMC Quality is a 0–100 score for each SMC setup, built from displacement, zone or sweep quality, room to the opposing liquidity in R, and path efficiency.

  10. The Runner after TP2: keeping part of the trade for the trend

    The Runner is the 25% of the position kept after TP2, with its stop locked at TP1 and then trailing the Trailing line, to capture trends that run beyond 2R.

  11. JSON alerts and webhooks: sending AURORA signals to a bot

    AURORA has two kinds of alert: setup alerts for entering trades yourself, and JSON alerts sent by webhook so a bot can read the entry, stop, targets and de-duplication ID of each event.

  12. Strategy Validator: testing AURORA with costs and realistic fills

    The Strategy Validator is AURORA's strategy version: the same signals run in TradingView's Strategy Tester with risk-based sizing, next-bar fills, commission and slippage.

  13. Data provenance: knowing where your chart's numbers come from

    Data provenance means knowing how each number on a chart is produced: real exchange volume, a broker's tick volume, reference volume from another market, or an estimate.
