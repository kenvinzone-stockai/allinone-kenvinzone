What goes into a trade plan?

A trade plan is a set of decisions locked in before entry: entry price, stop loss, size, where to take profit and when to exit early.

BeginnerAURORA StandardAURORA Pro9 min read
Topic: Risk & Position SizingMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

If price moves your way for a while and then turns, do you already know what you will do?

After this lesson you will

  • List the five parts of a trade plan
  • Calculate R results for a plan closing 50% at TP1 and 50% at TP2
  • Understand fixed stops versus moving the stop to breakeven

What is a trade plan?

A trade plan is five decisions made before entry:

  1. Entry: where you enter, with a market order or a limit order.
  2. Stop loss: the price at which the trade idea is considered wrong. The distance from entry to stop is 1R.
  3. Size: worked back from the money you accept to lose for 1R (position sizing).
  4. Take profit: one or more take-profit levels, and what share of the position closes at each.
  5. Other exit rules: what makes you close before the stop or target, such as a maximum holding time or a trend reversal.

With all five in place, there is no “what now?” question while the trade is running.

Why decide before entering?

  • Once in a trade, you are no longer objective. When losing you want to wait; when winning you fear giving it back. Deciding first avoids both reflexes.
  • Statistics become meaningful. If every trade exits differently, win rate and average R no longer describe your system.
  • Risk is capped in advance. Size comes from the stop, so one loss cannot exceed what you accepted.

Where do traders go wrong when planning?

  • A stop but no take-profit plan. Winners are held until they turn into losers, or closed too early out of fear.
  • Targets set by feel. Targets should be in R so you know whether the potential reward justifies the risk.
  • Widening the stop when price moves against you. The loss is now larger than the original 1R.
  • Changing rules mid-stream. Breakeven today, not tomorrow: the final results reflect no rule at all.

Chart example

Illustration, not real market data.

A common plan: close 50% of the position at TP1 (+1R) and 50% at TP2 (+2R). The result depends on whether, after TP1, you keep the stop or move it to breakeven:

What happens Keep the stop Move to breakeven after TP1
TP1, then TP2 +1.5R +1.5R
TP1, then back to the stop 0.5 × 1R − 0.5 × 1R = 0R 0.5 × 1R + 0 = +0.5R
Stop before TP1 −1R −1R
TP1, then an early exit at +1.3R 0.5 × 1R + 0.5 × 1.3R = +1.15R +1.15R

Breakeven protects the remaining position, but it also closes many trades on normal pullbacks before they reach TP2. Neither choice is right in every case; what matters is choosing in advance and staying consistent.

How does AURORA draw the trade plan?

Every signal that is entered in AURORA Standard and AURORA Pro comes with a plan drawn on the chart: a red risk box, a green target box, and Entry, F-SL, TP1 and TP2 labels.

  • F-SL is the fixed stop set at the signal candle. For BREAK and SUNNY it defaults to the Trailing line value at the candle close. T-SL is the dynamic stop following the current Trailing line, a reference for manual management.
  • Defaults: TP1 = 1R, TP2 = 2R, closing 50% / 50%.
  • After TP1: BREAK and SUNNY setups keep the F-SL by default, as in the original setup. Smart signals move to breakeven by default. In AURORA Pro, the Scalping and Prop Firm Trading Styles turn on breakeven for setups too.
  • Early exits, Adaptive mode (default): EXIT when the Trailing line flips, TIME when the maximum holding time runs out (200 bars by default), REV on an opposite signal, NEXT when a new same-direction Break takes over.
  • Result labels are written in R, for example ✓ +1.5R · TP2 or ■ 0R · SL, and the dashboard adds up results for each setup.

You do not need AURORA to have a trade plan: one note line with the five parts above is enough.

When does the plan not play out as drawn?

  • Gaps and slippage. Price can jump through the stop; the real loss exceeds 1R.
  • Sizes that cannot be split. With one futures contract or a minimum lot size you cannot close 50%; the plan must become a single full exit.
  • Statistics exclude costs. Chart results are historical and before commission and slippage. Use the Strategy Validator for figures after costs.
  • Every market is different. A 1R / 2R plan that suits gold H1 may not suit crypto M5. Check it on the exact symbol and timeframe you trade.

Practical checklist

  • Write down entry, stop, TP1, TP2 and size before placing the order.
  • Decide in advance: after TP1, keep the stop or move it to breakeven.
  • Decide in advance your early-exit reasons: time limit, trend reversal, opposite signal.
  • Do not change the plan mid-trade out of emotion; if you change it, record why.
  • Record every result in R so it can be compared with the plan.
