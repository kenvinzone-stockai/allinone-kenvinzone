Win rate vs Avg R: which number matters more?

Win rate only means something alongside the average win and loss; average R per trade (expectancy) combines both and decides whether a system has an edge.

IntermediateAURORA StandardAURORA Pro7 min read
Topic: Research & ValidationMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

Is a system that wins 70% of its trades necessarily better than one that wins only 40%?

After this lesson you will

  • Calculate expectancy per trade in R
  • Know the breakeven win rate for each reward:risk ratio
  • Read the win rate of a partial take-profit plan correctly

What are win rate and Avg R?

  • Win rate: the percentage of trades that make money.
  • Average R (Avg R): the average result per trade, in R.

Average R is the expectancy per trade:

Expectancy = Win rate × Average win − Loss rate × Average loss (in R)

For example, a system winning 70% with an average win of 0.3R and an average loss of 1R: 0.7 × 0.3 − 0.3 × 1 = −0.09R per trade. Lots of wins, still losing money.

The breakeven win rate

For a given ratio of average win to average loss (reward : risk), the minimum win rate to break even is:

Breakeven win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward:risk)

Average win : average loss Breakeven win rate
1 : 1 50%
1.5 : 1 40%
2 : 1 33.3%
3 : 1 25%

These figures are before costs; commission and slippage push the required win rate higher.

Common misunderstandings

  • A high win rate means a good system. Without the average win, the number means nothing.
  • Below 50% wins is poor. Many trend-following systems win 35–45% and profit because winners are bigger than losers.
  • Total profit is enough. Totals depend on trade count; compare systems by average R.
  • One good month proves it. You need enough trades; see the Sample size article.

How do you read these numbers in AURORA?

AURORA’s default plan closes 50% at TP1 (1R) and 50% at TP2 (2R) and keeps the stop, so each trade has three main outcomes: +1.5R, 0R (TP1 then back to the stop) and −1R. An example with hypothetical numbers:

Outcome Share Contribution
+1.5R (TP2 reached) 40% +0.60R
0R (TP1 then stop) 15% 0
−1R (stop) 45% −0.45R
Expectancy +0.15R per trade

The dashboard would show a 40% win rate, because 0R trades do not count as wins, yet the system still has positive expectancy. In practice there are also early exits (EXIT, TIME…) with fractional results; the dashboard includes them all in the Avg figure.

Reading order on the dashboard: trade count → average R (the Total R · Avg row) → how trades exited → win rate last.

Limits

  • Expectancy comes from the past. It changes with market phases.
  • You need enough trades. +0.15R over 30 trades cannot yet be told apart from 0.
  • Costs are excluded when read from the dashboard; use the Strategy Validator for figures after costs.

Practical checklist

  • Always read win rate together with the average win and average loss.
  • Calculate expectancy per trade in R, after costs.
  • Compare the actual win rate with the system's breakeven win rate.
  • With partial exits, remember 0R trades do not count as wins.
  • Never pick a system just for its high win rate.

A system that wins 70% of its trades sounds attractive. But if each win makes only 0.3R and each loss costs a full 1R, it still loses money. The deciding number is expectancy per trade: win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss, all in R. From that comes the breakeven win rate. With wins equal to losses you need 50%. With wins twice the size of losses you need only about 33%. With AURORA's partial take-profit plan, a system that wins only 40% can still have positive expectancy. So read average R first and win rate last.
