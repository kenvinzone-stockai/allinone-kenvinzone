What is a trend?

A trend is price's dominant direction on a given timeframe, usually described by a sequence of rising highs and lows (up) or falling highs and lows (down).

The question

Looking at the same chart, why does one trader see an uptrend and another a downtrend?

After this lesson you will

  • Describe a trend by swing structure (HH / HL, LH / LL)
  • Understand why a trend always belongs to a specific timeframe
  • Know when a trend counts as broken

What is a trend?

A trend is price’s dominant direction over a period of time. There are two common ways to define it:

1. By swing structure.

  • Uptrend: each high above the previous high (HH), each low above the previous low (HL).
  • Downtrend: lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL).
  • Range: highs and lows with no clear direction.

2. By an indicator rule. For example, price above a long-term moving average, or above a trailing stop line. This is more objective, but it lags.

Why does the trend matter?

  • Trading with the trend means being on the same side as the dominant flow; counter-trend trades usually need stronger reasons.
  • It defines invalidation. In an uptrend, the latest higher low is the level that, if broken, puts the trend in doubt, and a sensible place for a stop.
  • It decides the setup type. Buying a pullback in an uptrend is very different from picking a bottom in a downtrend.

Common misunderstandings

  • A trend is a single truth. A trend depends on the timeframe: H1 can be falling while D1 is still rising.
  • One red candle ends an uptrend. Pullbacks are normal; a trend breaks only when its structure breaks.
  • Eyeballing is enough. Each look can produce different highs and lows. Fixed rules keep you consistent.
  • The trend will last. Every trend ends; what matters is having an exit rule.

How does AURORA define the trend?

AURORA uses both layers, with fixed rules:

  • The long-term trend cycle: a long-term trend filter decides whether the market is in an up or down cycle. BREAK, SUNNY and SMC setups trade only in that direction.
  • The short-term trend: the direction of the Kenvin Super Trailing line, which flips when price closes through it.
  • Multi-timeframe agreement: the Score counts the share of higher timeframes pointing the same way, using closed higher-timeframe bars only.

You do not need AURORA to apply this: marking HH / HL on your trading timeframe and one higher timeframe is enough to start.

When does a trend count as broken?

  • By structure: in an uptrend, a candle closes below the latest higher low. This is often called a break of structure (BOS) in the opposite direction.
  • By an indicator rule: price closes through the trend line you use.
  • Limits: both methods lag, and in ranging markets both give many false reversals.

Practical checklist

  • State the timeframe whenever you talk about a trend.
  • Mark the latest swing high and swing low to define the structure.
  • Choose one trend definition and stay consistent.
  • Decide in advance which level, if broken, ends the trend.
  • Trade with the higher-timeframe trend when you can.
