Multi-timeframe context

Multi-timeframe analysis reads the trend on higher timeframes for context, then uses the trading timeframe to time entries in that direction.

BeginnerAURORA StandardAURORA Pro6 min read
Topic: Trading FoundationsMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

H1 says buy while D1 is falling hard: which timeframe should you trust?

After this lesson you will

  • Choose a sensible context timeframe and trading timeframe
  • Understand why higher timeframes deserve more weight
  • Avoid using unfinished higher-timeframe bars when studying the past

What is multi-timeframe analysis?

The same market tells a different story on each timeframe. Multi-timeframe analysis splits the job in two:

  • The context timeframe (higher): where is the main trend going? Should you favour buying or selling?
  • The trading timeframe (where you enter): where to enter, where to put the stop?

A common rule of thumb: the context timeframe is about 4–6 times the trading timeframe, for example H1 for M15, H4 for H1, D1 for H4.

Why does it matter?

  • Avoid buying into a big downtrend. An H1 buy while D1 falls hard is often just a bounce.
  • Higher-timeframe trends are more durable. A D1 candle contains many hours of trading, so short-term noise rarely flips it.
  • Combine precision with context. The low timeframe gives the entry and a close stop; the high timeframe gives the direction.

Common misunderstandings

  • More timeframes is better. Watching too many leads to conflict and no decision.
  • Every timeframe counts equally. M5 agreement means much less than D1 agreement.
  • Lower timeframes give context. Timeframes below your trading timeframe are just noise inside the candle you are watching.
  • Using the live higher-timeframe bar to test the past. This is the most common look-ahead mistake in backtesting.

How does AURORA measure timeframe agreement?

  • Nine default timeframes: 1m, 3m, 5m, 8m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 4h, 1D (editable in the Extra Timeframes group).
  • Two criteria per timeframe: the direction of the Kenvin cloud and of the main trend.
  • Default mode Higher TFs (weighted):
    • Only timeframes above the chart count.
    • Up to 4× the chart: weight ×1; up to 24×: ×2; larger: ×3.
    • The legacy All 9 TFs mode counts all nine equally.
  • Only closed higher-timeframe bars are used, so past results do not use future information.
  • Display:
    • The dashboard has an MTF (HTF) row with the buy / sell agreement percentage.
    • The Full layout adds the 9-timeframe table, with uncounted timeframes dimmed.
    • The LIVE panel (Mini) shows HTF %.
  • In the Score: timeframe agreement is part of the Trend family.

Limits of multi-timeframe analysis

  • Lag: higher timeframes turn slowly; by the time they confirm a reversal, price may have moved far.
  • Conflict is normal: during a pullback, the lower timeframe runs against the higher one for a while.
  • The 4–6× ratio is a rule of thumb. Test it on the market and timeframe you trade.

Practical checklist

  • Pick a context timeframe about 4–6 times your trading timeframe.
  • Prefer trading in the context timeframe's direction.
  • Use the trading timeframe to choose the entry and place the stop.
  • When higher and lower timeframes conflict, trade smaller or stand aside.
  • Use only closed higher-timeframe bars when judging context.
