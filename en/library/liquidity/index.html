Where is the liquidity on a chart?

On a chart, liquidity usually means areas where resting orders cluster around obvious highs and lows; price often sweeps them before continuing or reversing.

IntermediateAURORA Pro7 min read
Topic: Trend & Market StructureMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

Why does a stop placed just below an old low so often get tagged by a few ticks before price bounces?

After this lesson you will

  • Recognise chart areas likely to hold clusters of resting orders
  • Understand liquidity sweeps and why they are not always traps
  • Know the limits of inferring liquidity from candlestick charts

What is liquidity on a chart?

In technical analysis, especially in Smart Money Concepts (SMC), liquidity usually refers to price areas likely to hold many resting orders:

  • Below obvious lows: stops of long trades and sell-stop breakout orders.
  • Above obvious highs: stops of short trades and buy-stop breakout orders.
  • Equal highs and equal lows: many traders see the same level, so orders pile up there.

When price reaches these areas, the resting orders trigger. For a large player wanting to buy size, a cascade of sell stops below a low is a source of supply to fill against.

Why does it matter?

  • Better stop placement. A stop right below an obvious low is easily tagged by an ordinary sweep.
  • Better breakout reading. A poke through a high that closes back inside is very different from a close beyond the high that holds.
  • Understanding why first breakouts often fail. This is also why AURORA tracks the 2nd BREAK separately.

Common misunderstandings

  • Every wick is a stop hunt. A wick can be ordinary volatility. A sweep means something when it happens at an obvious level and the candle closes back inside.
  • You can know exactly where liquidity sits. A candlestick chart does not show the order book; liquidity zones are inferred.
  • A sweep is always a reversal signal. In a strong trend, price can sweep a low and keep falling. Reading sweeps in the main trend direction is safer.
  • SMC is how institutions trade. SMC is a way of interpreting charts; nothing guarantees that large funds actually trade these patterns.

How does AURORA Pro use the idea of liquidity?

AURORA Pro does not eyeball liquidity; it uses fixed rules:

  • Swings are confirmed after 3 bars on each side and do not repaint.
  • SMC SWEEP + MSS: a wick sweeps the pullback swing, the candle closes back inside, then a candle closes beyond the latest high (low) within 8 bars.
  • Tier 3: a sweep of a recent high / low (searched over 15 bars) just before a BREAK turns the label from ⚡ to 💎.
  • SMC Quality: measures the room to the opposing liquidity, the nearest unbroken swing ahead, in R. More room means a higher score.

Every liquidity-based setup in AURORA Pro trades only in the main trend direction.

Limits of the liquidity concept

  • Inferred, not observed. “Stop clusters” are a reasonable assumption, not visible data.
  • Hindsight makes it look obvious. On a past chart every sweep looks clear; in real time it does not.
  • Markets behave differently. Thin stocks, forex and crypto react differently to the same pattern.
  • Validate it. Measure liquidity-based setups on your own data before trusting them. Historical results, before costs.

Practical checklist

  • Mark obvious highs and lows, especially equal highs and equal lows.
  • Avoid placing stops right below an obvious low or above an obvious high.
  • Wait for the close: a wick through and back differs from a close through.
  • Read sweeps in the direction of the main trend; do not pick bottoms against it.
  • Remember liquidity is inferred from the chart, not read from a real order book.
