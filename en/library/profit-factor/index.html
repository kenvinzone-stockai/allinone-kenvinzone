What is Profit Factor?

Profit Factor is gross profit divided by gross loss over the tested period; above 1 means profitable on that data, but it must be read with sample size, drawdown and costs.

IntermediateAURORA StandardAURORA Pro6 min read
Topic: Research & ValidationMarket: All marketsAuthor: KenvinHoangReviewer: KapyBara✓ Reviewed 14 Oct 2026
The question

A Profit Factor of 2.0 over 15 trades or 1.3 over 400 trades: which is more trustworthy?

After this lesson you will

  • Calculate Profit Factor from gross profit and gross loss
  • Understand how Profit Factor relates to win rate and average R
  • Know why Profit Factor on small samples misleads

What is Profit Factor?

Profit Factor = Gross profit of winning trades ÷ Gross loss of losing trades

  • Above 1: profitable on the tested data.
  • Equal to 1: breakeven.
  • Below 1: losing.

Profit Factor is closely related to expectancy: both combine win rate with the size of wins and losses. The difference is that Profit Factor is a ratio, while expectancy is a difference in R per trade.

Example

Hypothetical system: 40% of trades +1.5R, 15% 0R, 45% −1R.

Before costs After 0.1R cost per trade
Gross profit per trade 0.40 × 1.5 = 0.60R 0.40 × 1.4 = 0.56R
Gross loss per trade 0.45 × 1 = 0.45R 0.45 × 1.1 + 0.15 × 0.1 = 0.51R
Profit Factor 1.33 about 1.10
Expectancy +0.15R +0.05R

A cost of 0.1R per trade sounds small, yet it removes two thirds of the expectancy.

Common misunderstandings

  • A high Profit Factor means a good system. On small samples, a few big winners can push it very high.
  • The higher it is, the more you should trust it. A suspiciously good number (say above 3) on short data is often a sign of overfitting or luck.
  • Profit Factor describes risk. No. Two systems with the same Profit Factor can have very different drawdowns.
  • Reading it before costs. Always compare the figure after commission and slippage.

How do you read Profit Factor with AURORA?

  • The Strategy Validator (Standard and Pro) shows Profit Factor in the Strategy Tester, after the commission and slippage set in Strategy Properties.
  • The chart dashboard does not show Profit Factor; it shows total and average R, before costs.
  • Comparing configurations: when changing the Entry Filter Profile or a filter, compare Profit Factor, average R, maximum drawdown and trade count together. Never choose a configuration just for the highest Profit Factor.
  • In-sample versus out-of-sample: a sharp drop in Profit Factor out-of-sample is a warning sign.

Limits

  • Sensitive to big trades. A single very large winner can make up most of the gross profit.
  • Says nothing about time. Profit Factor does not tell you how long the system took to earn it.
  • Past results do not guarantee the future.

Practical checklist

  • Read Profit Factor after costs, not before.
  • Always state the trade count alongside Profit Factor.
  • Check whether a few of the largest trades make up most of the profit.
  • Read it together with maximum drawdown.
  • Compare Profit Factor between in-sample and out-of-sample periods.
